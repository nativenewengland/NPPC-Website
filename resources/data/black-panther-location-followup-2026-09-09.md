# Follow-up on uncertain Black Panther locations

Research completed 9 September 2026. This review revisits all seven leads held
out of the 86-location catalog. It adds **two locations**, bringing the map to
**88**. Neither an individual's Party membership nor a proposal to form a group
is sufficient by itself to establish a local organization.

## Added: Soledad, California — underground prison chapter

Zoe Colley's peer-reviewed article, *War without Terms: George Jackson, Black
Power and the American Radical Prison Rights Movement, 1941–1971*, **History
101(345), 265–286 (2016), DOI 10.1111/1468-229X.12228**, explicitly describes
George Jackson and W. L. Nolen forming an underground Party chapter at Soledad
in late 1969. The sentence crosses **PDF pages 22–23** of the
[author manuscript held by the University of Dundee](https://discovery.dundee.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/11996627/War_Without_Terms_Main_Document.pdf#page=22).
Both pages were visually checked.

Colley's footnote 58 cites Lori Andrews, *Black Power, White Blood: The Life and
Times of Johnny Spain*, p. 111. That book page was not independently inspected;
the map cites Colley's explicit published finding. The label preserves
“underground,” and does not assert a public office, exact founding day or a
separately located headquarters.

## Added: Paterson, New Jersey — local Panther organizing group

The earlier review found only the
[Paterson Museum's 1968 fundraiser and chapter-recruitment entry](https://patersonmuseum.com/african-american-history-timeline/).
The follow-up traced a stronger claim through
[a Youngstown State thesis, printed p. 60, footnote 143](https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/ws/send_file/send?accession=ysu1197081489&disposition=inline)
to **David Boesel, Louis C. Goldberg and Gary T. Marx, “Rebellion in Plainfield,”
in Cities Under Siege (1971), p. 83**.

The [original scan on coauthor Marx's MIT website, PDF p. 17](https://www.mit.edu/~gtmarx/rebellion_in_plainfield.pdf#page=17)
was downloaded and visually checked. It identifies Panthers from Paterson
helping organize Plainfield in summer 1968. The source's Plainfield title is
therefore not a mistaken citation for the Paterson connection. However, its
wording is narrower than the later thesis's chapter claim. Together with the
museum record, it supports an active local organizing presence, which is the
map label. **Formal chapter recognition, an office address and an exact
founding date remain unverified.** Paterson is grouped with organizing
locations, not added to the count of confirmed chapter locations.

## Charlotte, North Carolina — AAUO recognition effort, no addition

The [State Library's NCpedia history](https://www.ncpedia.org/black-panther-party)
identifies the Afro-American Unity Organization and explicitly reports that
it failed to obtain Oakland's official recognition. A contemporary
[May 19, 1969 Charlotte FBI teletype, file 157-6527, in BPP Part 1](https://vault.fbi.gov/Black%20Panther%20Party%20/Black%20Panther%20Party%20Part%2001/at_download/file)
also names AAUO, Benjamin Chavis and Jerome Johnson; its indexed text describes
Panther-style dress rather than proving affiliation. The state history is the
basis for withholding a chapter marker. An FBI field office named Charlotte
must not be mistaken for a Panther organization in Charlotte.

## St. Petersburg, Florida — JOMO is distinct, no addition

The [December 1, 1970 Tampa memorandum, PDF p. 13](https://blackfreedom.proquest.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/blackpanther11.pdf#page=13)
discusses Tampa's NCCF and St. Petersburg's JOMO separately. A further
[Burning Spear account of Omali Yeshitela's political development](https://theburningspear.com/uneasy-equilibrium-new-book-reflects-development-from-joe-waller-to-omali-yeshitela/)
describes JOMO as an organization with similarities to the Oakland Panthers.
This participant-organization account strengthens the distinction; it does
not establish that JOMO was an original-party chapter. No marker is added on
the strength of this lead. This does not rule out other Panther activity in
the city.

## Las Vegas, Nevada — original-era inquiry found; operation unconfirmed

Historian Robyn C. Spencer's
[Duke University Press essay of January 26, 2017](https://dukeupress.wordpress.com/2017/01/26/the-black-panther-party-and-black-anti-fascism-in-the-united-states/)
identifies Las Vegas among places that sent inquiries about starting NCCFs
after the July 1969 United Front Against Fascism conference. This supplies an
original-era lead, but the essay does not say the Las Vegas group opened.

The [Ron Current oral-history catalog at UNLV](https://special.library.unlv.edu/ark%3A/62930/d1xp6vg3g)
does not settle that chronology in the accessible indexed material, while
[March 25, 2001 reporting](https://www.deseret.com/2001/3/25/19576946/rash-of-gang-shootings-plagues-las-vegas-neighborhood/)
identifies Current with the **New** Black Panther Party. The full UNLV item
could not be retrieved during this review; it is not presented as fully read.
**Still needed:** evidence that the 1969 inquiry led to an operating NCCF or
original-party unit, such as correspondence confirming acceptance, a directory
listing, or an account of its programs. Current's later group does not fill
that gap.

## Ithaca, New York — proposed NCCF, no confirmed opening

The [December 4, 1970 Albany memorandum, PDF p. 15](https://blackfreedom.proquest.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/blackpanther11.pdf#page=15)
uses conditional language about efforts to organize a group. The
[April 25, 1970 Party newspaper, PDF p. 8](https://washingtonareaspark.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/1970-04-25-black-panther-vol-4-no-21.pdf#page=8)
reports on Ithaca's Black community; newspaper coverage does not identify a
local Party unit by itself.

The [Cornell finding aid for collection 3650](https://rmc.library.cornell.edu/EAD/htmldocs/RMM03650.html)
identifies concrete archival targets: **Box 1, Folder 9, “Information on
Ithaca,” 1968–1971; Box 2, Folder 8, “Ithaca, June–July 1970”; and Box 1,
Folders 3–4, Fall 1970 / Spring 1971**. The finding aid was read, but the
undigitized contents were not. These are retrieval leads, not evidence of a
chapter. **Still needed:** a dated opening, program, office or affiliation
record after the proposed organizing effort.

## Folsom, California — imprisoned Panthers, no verified local chapter

The [1973 Warren Wells interview catalog and public transcript at AAPB](https://americanarchive.org/catalog/cpb-aacip-28-z31ng4h97d)
document an imprisoned former Panther discussing conditions and outside
support. The catalog lists a June 4, 1973 broadcast; its transcript is explicitly
unverified. No chapter claim is adopted from it, and the audio was not audited.
The [December 4, 1971 Party newspaper, printed p. 14](https://www.marxists.org/history/usa/pubs/black-panther/07%20no%2015%201-20%20dec%204%201971.pdf)
also contains David Hilliard's Folsom prison correspondence. These establish
individuals' imprisonment and political connections, not a Folsom chapter.
**Still needed:** an explicit local-unit statement, officers, charter or
chapter correspondence comparable to the evidence found for Soledad.

## Additional lead: Lumberton, North Carolina

The indexed text of the [May 16, 1974 Charlotte memorandum, file 157-9763,
in FBI BPP Part 33](https://vault.fbi.gov/Black%20Panther%20Party%20/Black%20Panther%20Party%20Part%2033/at_download/file)
describes a group formed in Lumberton on February 20 by two former Winston-Salem
members, explicitly calling it independent of other organizations. The scan
could not be retrieved for a visual check. This is a lead for separate research
into that group's identity, not grounds to add an original-party chapter.
Government descriptions are attributed as evidence, not adopted as judgments
of political legitimacy.

## Map data and limits

Soledad uses Census GEOID **0672520**, coordinates **36.434522, -121.317759**.
Paterson uses GEOID **3457000**, coordinates **40.914766, -74.162829**. Both
match the [2025 Census place Gazetteer](https://www2.census.gov/geo/docs/maps-data/data/gazetteer/2025_Gazetteer/2025_Gaz_place_national.zip)
exactly. They are modern city points, not asserted historical office or prison
building coordinates. All earlier location records and timeline milestones
are preserved. The map remains a sourced inventory rather than an assertion
of complete historical coverage.
