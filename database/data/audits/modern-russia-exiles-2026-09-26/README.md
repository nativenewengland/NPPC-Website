# Exiles to modern Russia — initial pass, September 26, 2026

This pass begins with Americans whose exile, asylum or refugee status in the Russian Federation after the Soviet collapse can be independently documented. It distinguishes the person’s stated persecution claim from verified criminal charges and from the Russian government’s legal status.

## Publication status

Published September 26, 2026. John Anthony Robles II, John Mark Dougan and Tara Reade were added as new profiles. Edward Snowden’s existing profile was updated with sources and the dated sequence from temporary asylum through permanent residence and Russian citizenship. The live database check passed and all four public profile URLs returned HTTP 200.

## Published records

- **John Anthony Robles II** — moved to Russia in 1996; U.S. passport renewal denied in 2007 over certified child-support arrears; received Russian refugee status; granted Russian citizenship October 21, 2024. His claims of political and CIA retaliation remain attributed rather than accepted as fact.
- **John Mark Dougan** — fled after a March 2016 FBI and local-police search; later charged in Florida with 21 counts involving extortion and illegal recording; received temporary Russian asylum in February 2017 and permanent status in December 2018.
- **Tara Reade** — moved to Russia in May 2023, claimed U.S. government threats and later said she received asylum; the White House denied that her life was endangered by the government; granted Russian citizenship September 22, 2025.
- **Edward Snowden** — the existing profile now contains sourced dates for temporary asylum, permanent residency and Russian citizenship, while preserving the pending U.S. charges and the fact that he has not been arrested or imprisoned on them.

## Screened or deferred

- **Wilmer Puello-Mota** fled in January 2024 immediately before an expected guilty plea on child-sexual-abuse-image and obstruction charges and joined the Russian military. No asylum decision or supported political-persecution claim was located. This is documented criminal flight, not currently a political-exile profile.
- **John McIntyre** joined Ukraine’s foreign legion and later crossed to Russia after saying he had gathered intelligence for Russian forces. Available reporting describes wartime defection, not exile from U.S. prosecution or a Russian asylum case.
- **Russell Bentley** voluntarily moved to Russian-controlled Donetsk in 2014 and joined separatist forces. No American prosecution or coercive departure trigger was located.
- **Leo and Chantel Haer and their children** received temporary asylum in 2024 after moving for stated “traditional values” reasons. The available evidence describes voluntary ideological migration rather than persecution in the United States.
- **Joseph Tater** said he came to seek asylum because the CIA persecuted him, but no corroboration or grant was located. Russian authorities instead detained him and ordered psychiatric treatment while prosecuting an alleged assault.

Russian migration statistics show that the formal “political asylum” category was essentially unused after 1992; reported American cases generally involve refugee status, temporary asylum, residence permits or discretionary citizenship. From 2011 through 2021, Russia accepted 22 temporary-asylum applications from U.S. citizens and granted six. Approvals increased after 2022, but most recipients were not publicly named.

Principal overview: [Civic Assistance Committee, migration statistics for U.S. citizens in Russia](https://refugee.ru/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/overview-of-migration-statistics-for-us-citizens-in-the-russian-federation.docx.pdf).
