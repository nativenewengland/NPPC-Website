# Iraqi refugee living in Richmond pleads guilty to aiding ISIS hacking group

By John Wayne Ferguson, Staff writer March 11, 2025

Bob Casey United States Court House for the Southern District of Texas is photographed on Friday, Dec. 1, 2023 in Houston.

Yi-Chin Lee/Staff Photographer

An Iraqi refugee living in Richmond has pleaded guilty to conspiracy charges over an accusation that for years he helped ISIS by making logos for the terrorist organization's computer hacking group.

Abdulrahman Mohammed Hafedh Alqaysi, 28, pleaded guilty last week to conspiracy to provide material support to a designated foreign terrorist organization, according to the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of Texas.

Alqaysi in 2022 was accused of supporting the Kalachnikov E-Security Team, a part of the ISIS "cyber army" that worked to recruit people online, deface websites and hack social media, among other crimes.

According to court records, Alqaysi created logos for Kalachnikov, and passed them around to other people to be placed on hacked accounts and websites. Investigators said they also intercepted texts between Mohammed Alqaysi and Abdulrahman Alqaysi, who are cousins. In one of the texts, Abdulrahman Alqaysi called himself "the hacker of the State" and passed along pictures of the ISIS flag, according to court records.

Prosecutors said they also had messages showing Alqaysi texting with ISIS members in 2015. In one message, he said he wanted to use ISIS publications to "create a game for the Islamic State." In another, he was asked to recruit people into the terrorist group, according to court records.

Alqaysi is described in court records as a Iraqi refugee who became a legal permanent resident in 2016 and was living in Richmond.

As part of his plea agreement, Alqaysi promised to cooperate with investigators if asked, and to possibly testify to a grand jury. The government promised to dismiss other charges against Alqaysi and said it could recommend a reduced sentence to a federal judge, if Alqaysi demonstrates acceptance of responsibility for his crimes.

Alqaysi will be sentenced in June. He could be sentenced to up to 20 years in prison and fined up to $250,000.

## About

Mark’s artistic roots started at a young age. His talent was encouraged by many including his father and HS art instructors. This led to furthering his art education and ultimately a BFA degree from Northern Arizona University (NAU). His first job in the creative field was as a graphic designer for a large advertising agency in Tucson, Arizona where he quickly learned many skills that would benefit him later in business. He was later hired by the University of Arizona’s (UA) medical illustration department which added another dimension to his talent. Subsequently, he became the Art Director for the City of Tucson where he earned several creative awards. It was at this juncture that he decided to pursue his own business in the creative field and opened up Tucci Creative, Inc., an art studio/advertising agency venture. Mark also taught for a brief time at Southwest University of Visual Arts (SUVA) in the early stages of his new endeavor.

Mark’s first major client was Old Tucson Studios, the famous western movie film set and location in Tucson, Arizona, where he illustrated and designed hundreds of print ads published around the world. At the same time, Mark was also the exclusive illustrator for American West Magazine, a national publication which published his editorial drawings for more than 8 years. This opened up the doors to producing larger commissioned, illustrative works for companies like Ruger Firearms, Pentax and AT&T, among others.

Mark was also commissioned to illustrate 3 cookbooks: State Fair Recipes by Catherine Hanley, Southwestern Kitchen by Jane Butel, and Country Tastes by Beatrice Ojakangas.

On the agency side, he began to have a lot of success creating radio and television commercials and was the driving force behind the creative process; writing, directing and producing thousands of advertisements throughout his 30+ years in the agency business. Tucci Creative, Inc., the agency, garnished over 40 local and regional ADDY awards along the way for their television, radio and print creative.

Mark’s love of drawing has never waned. More recently, he’s been consumed with this lifelong passion along with other artistic ambitions such as sculpture and oil painting. Having been fortunate enough to travel around the world, drawing became Mark’s camera, sketching in pen & ink urban scenes and country landscapes wherever he went without ever using a pencil as a preliminary draft. Some of the drawings you see here were drawn entirely on-site and have a spontaneous, energetic and engaging quality that allows the viewer to see these “snap shots” of life through the artist’s own eyes. HIs favorite subject is the human figure and you will see many of his illustrations include it.

This website contains some of Mark’s favorite illustrations over the past 15 years. Please enjoy your time viewing them and send a comment if you wish. Prints are now available to purchase for the first time ever!

Thanks to all who have supported Mark over the years. His dedication to his craft has a lot to do with you and your love of his work.--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Report: ICE Detains Hasidic Israeli Who Took Part in Neturei Karta Anti-Israel Protests

October 1, 2026 10:22 pm

6

Yehonatan Ovadia, at left, carries a Palestinian flag during an anti-Israel demonstration with other anti-Zionist Hasidic Jews displaying signs associated with Neturei Karta. (Photo: Eric Lee)

NEW YORKM (VINnews) – A Hasidic Israeli man who participated in anti-Israel protests alongside members of Neturei Karta is facing deportation from the United States after being detained by Immigration and Customs Enforcement, according to the Jewish Telegraphic Agency.

Yehonatan Ovadia, 35, an Orthodox Jew from the anti-Zionist Satmar Hasidic sect, is originally from Israel. He moved to the United States in 2022 on a temporary religious-worker visa and participated in protests in New York and New Jersey against Israel, JTA reported.

Ovadia was detained by ICE in August and remains in custody in New York. His immigration status was revoked in connection with his participation in a November 2023 protest outside the Ramapo, New York, town hall, where an Israeli flag was removed.

### Independent photographer released from jail after 47 days in detention

Trisha Brownlee, an independent photographer who was arrested while reporting on immigration enforcement and detained for over six weeks, was released from the Warren County Correctional Center in Belvedere, New Jersey, on July 27, 2026.

Brownlee had been interviewing two men who were observing Immigration and Customs Enforcement officers from their vehicle in Hardwick Township on June 11 when New Jersey State Police were called. The trio was subsequently arrested for unregistered weapons in the vehicle.

Brownlee was charged with illegal possession of armor-piercing rounds and kept in jail for 47 days. Her mother, Sharon Edwards, told the U.S. Press Freedom Tracker that she went without her medication for Crohn’s disease during that time.

The state prosecutor filed a motion for release on July 24 following “extensive discussions,” a spokesperson for Brownlee’s public defender told the Tracker. A judge ruled three days later that Brownlee could be released, according to court filings.

Brownlee will need to check in twice a month with pretrial services, according to the spokesperson. She added that Brownlee’s cellphone remains in custody with the state police.

Edwards told the Tracker that under the agreement, Brownlee is required to stay in Pennsylvania, where she had temporarily been living with her mother before her arrest, and will receive medical treatment at the local Veterans Affairs hospital.

Brownlee’s next hearing is on Sept. 23.
