# Among USF Gaza protesters: a past supporter of Palestinian Islamic Jihad

ImageBy Ian HodgsonJune 17, 2024June 21, 2024

Image Hatem Naji Fariz, center with bullhorn, leads a pro-Palestinian rally Oct. 25, 2023, in Tampa. [ LUIS SANTANA | Tampa Bay Times ]

He’s been a regular at pro-Palestinian protests around Tampa: the stocky man with a neatly trimmed and graying beard.

Over the past six months, he’s led chants demanding that the University of South Florida divest from Israeli-linked companies. He’s handed out homemade signs calling for the end of Israel’s war with Hamas. He’s led prayers for peace and freedom for the people of Gaza.

Hatem Naji Fariz, 51, is respected among other rally organizers. For local law enforcement, he’s considered a trusted member of Tampa’s Muslim community. But buried in decades-old court records, among the hundreds of pages of phone calls transcribed by federal law enforcement officials, is a far different version of Fariz.

More than 20 years ago — on Feb. 20, 2003 — federal agents raided Fariz’s Spring Hill home, arresting the then-30-year-old on charges that he provided financial support to Palestinian Islamic Jihad, a federally designated terrorist organization.

On phone calls recorded by federal agents, Fariz joked about a terrorist attack in Israel, explained how to obscure payments made to a terrorism-adjacent charity organization and bragged about beating a young man whom he suspected of collaborating with the Israelis, according to court records.

While Fariz was acquitted of most of the charges against him, he pleaded guilty to providing material support to a terrorist organization.

Fariz did not respond to multiple emails, phone calls and a letter left at his Tarpon Springs home. In an email to the Tampa Bay Times, Kevin Beck, Fariz’s former defense attorney, said “the government made frequent errors in describing the actions that led to the charges (against Fariz and his co-defendants).”

Beck did not address specific questions about the allegations or other court documents reviewed by the Times.

Image: Hatem Naji Fariz, center, is seen during a pro-Palestinian rally May 1 in Tampa.Hatem Naji Fariz, center, is seen during a pro-Palestinian rally May 1 in Tampa. [ LUIS SANTANA | Tampa Bay Times ]

For some community leaders who know of this long-ago case, Fariz has since proved himself to be a leader.

“Hatem has been a force for reason within the Muslim community,” said Temple Terrace police Chief Kenneth Albano. He said he’s known Fariz for more than a decade and has coordinated with him to ensure that recent demonstrations and events organized by Fariz were peaceful and respectful of the larger community.

Albano said he knew of Fariz’s prior conviction but added “you should look at the totality of everything if you’re trying to understand.”

When it comes to coordinating protests with Albano’s office, Fariz “shares information and listens to concerns,” Albano said. “He tries to speak reason into situations that could go differently.”


Among the nine men charged in 2003 with supporting the group was Al-Arian, a USF computer science professor whom federal prosecutors identified as the group’s leader.

In 2006, a jury acquitted Fariz on 25 of the 33 charges brought against him by federal prosecutors but couldn’t come to a unanimous verdict on the remaining eight.

After a 14-month trial, Fariz pleaded guilty to conspiracy to provide material support to a terrorist organization and agreed to a 37-month prison sentence. The remaining seven charges were dropped as part of Fariz’s plea agreement.

Image: Hatem Naji Fariz, center, leads a march along Fowler Avenue in support of the Palestinian people on Oct. 25, 2023, in Tampa.Hatem Naji Fariz, center, leads a march along Fowler Avenue in support of the Palestinian people on Oct. 25, 2023, in Tampa. [ LUIS SANTANA | Tampa Bay Times ]

The conviction came after an intensive federal investigation that showed over a two-year period Fariz wired more than $40,000 to charities in Gaza and the West Bank with direct ties to Palestinian Islamic Jihad, according to the indictment.

Concejo de Bogotá

PABLO CÉSAR PACHECO RODRÍGUEZ

Bogotano de nacimiento, abogado de la Universidad Nacional de Colombia, graduado en el año 2001, y especialista en Derecho Público de la Universidad Externado de Colombia.

 

Su primera experiencia profesional tuvo lugar en el Resguardo del pueblo Embera Katio del Alto Sinú, en el departamento de Córdoba, en donde ejerció labores de asesoría y acompañamiento a la comunidad indígena afectada por la construcción de la represa Urrá 1.

 

Después se ha desempeñado principalmente en el sector público. Entre los cargos ejercidos se cuentan: Asesor de Política Ambiental Local y Territorial en la Secretaría Distrital de Ambiente y consultor del Programa de Naciones Unidas para el Desarrollo - PNUD, en la implementación de la Política de Hábitat en Bogotá.

 

En el área de vivienda, también se ha desempeñado como abogado en Metrovivienda, hoy Empresa de Renovación y Desarrollo Urbano, en la ejecución y seguimiento del Subsidio Distrital de Vivienda. Luego en el mismo sector, asesoró a la Subdirección de Recursos Públicos de la Secretaria Distrital de Hábitat en instrumentos de financiación para el acceso a la Vivienda de Interés Social.

 

Desde finales del año 2010 hasta 2016, laboró como asesor y coordinador de una Unidad de Apoyo Normativo en el Concejo de Bogotá,  en donde ha participado en la formulación de Proyectos de Acuerdo en temas relacionados con medio ambiente, participación ciudadana y víctimas del conflicto armado. Por otro lado, respecto al Control Político realizó seguimiento a los sectores de ambiente, vivienda, movilidad y política social. En el desarrollo de su gestión participó en iniciativas para la reforma al reglamento del Cabildo Distrital.

Por los fundamentos que expondremos a continuación, se confirma la Sentencia apelada.

I.

Surge del expediente de la apelación ante nos, que el 23 de septiembre de 2020, el Ministerio Público presentó dos Denuncias en contra del apelante por violaciones al Art. 109 (agresión grave) del Código Penal de Puerto Rico (Código Penal), 33 LPRA sec 5162 y al Art, 6.06 de la Ley Núm. 168-2020, conocida como la Ley de Armas de Puerto Rico de 2020 (Ley de Armas), 25 LPRA sec, 466e, por hechos ocurridos el 10 de enero de 2020. Luego de celebrada la Vista de Determinación de Causa para Arresto, el Tribunal determinó causa por los delitos imputados.

# MST celebra 40 años de lucha por el socialismo

Image

Por

Redacción Bandera Roja

| Publicado el 16 agosto 2022

El pasado 13 de agosto el Movimiento Socialista de Trabajadoras y Trabajadores MST, celebró sus 40 años como organización socialista. La actividad se llevo a cabo en el Colegio de Abogadas y Abogados de Puerto Rico. El acto estuvo dedicado a Carlos Fortuño Candelas e incluyó presentaciones artísticas junto con 4 conferencias sobre el desarrollo histórico del MST.

En el primer mensaje el compañero Carlos Román Espada presento una semblanza dedicada a Carlos Fortuño Candelas. Mientras que Federico Fernández hablo sobre la fusión del MSP y el PSR, hasta la huelga del pueblo de 1998. El compañero Ricardo Santos Ortíz presento un balance del periodo de la lucha contra la marina en Vieques a la huelga magisterial y la actividad cerro con el mensaje de la compañera Isabel Berríos Cruz, que hablo sobre las huelgas universitarias 2010 y 2011, la lucha feminista, las luchas ambientales y las explosiones de lucha de clases como el verano combativo. Perspectiva de cara al futuro. La actividad contó con presentaciones de nuestrxs queridos cantautorxs Agustín Muñoz Ríos y Teresa Sepulveda.

Con una militancia fundamentalmente de clase trabajadora, en el MST encontramos un desarrollo autóctono de la política socialista en Puerto Rico, ligado íntimamente a los acontecimientos de la lucha de clases de los últimos 40 años. Partiendo de un activismo militante, sin desatender el análisis profundo de la realidad social, el MST sigue en pie de lucha sembrando consciencia de clase y organizando para lograr una nueva sociedad puertorriqueña feminista, anti racista, democrática, independiente, eco socialista y dirigida por la clase trabajadora.

L120: Posted on May 28, 2019 • by cite53†Seán Kinane L121: 
L122: cite54†Image: Sameeh Hammoudeh†cdn.wmnf.org Sameeh Hammoudeh. From his Facebook page.
L123: 
L124: Sameeh Hammoudeh, one of the four defendants in the Tampa trial of cite55†Sami Al-Arian has died; in 2005 Hammoudeh was found not guilty on all charges.
L125: 
L126: But after the acquittal Hammoudeh was cite56†not allowed to finish his graduate degree at the University of South Florida†l.facebook.com in Tampa.
L127: He went on to get his doctorate in Palestine and became the chair of the political science department at cite57†Birzeit University†www.facebook.com in Ramallah.
L128: 
L129: WMNF interviewed Mel Underbakke, who is a founder of Friends of Human Rights and attended much of the 2005 trial of Hammoudeh, Al-Arian and the other defendants in Tampa.
L130: > “There were four co-defendants in there. Sameeh Hammoudeh was one of them. He was totally acquitted [on all charges] as was Ghassan Ballut. And then two of them [Sami Al-Arian and Hatim Fariz] had pending charges that they could carry on [the jury was divided on some charges; the government could have re-tried them on those charges]. Even though all of [the co-defendants] were totally acquitted on terrorism charges.”
L131: #### SK: After he was acquitted, Sameeh Hammoudeh had to leave the country. Where did he end up?
L132: 
L133: > “In the West Bank. He finished his doctorate, I believe, there somewhere. He became the chair of a political science department at a university in the West Bank.”
L134: #### SK: But he died recently. What did he die of?
L135: 
L136: > “He died of cancer. It was very, very sad. He had family. I’ve never met Sameeh myself. I know him only from the courtroom. For six months we were in the courtroom every day. It was a totally unjust case.”

L87: cite41†Image: Dr. Abdulrahim Ahmad Al-Assaslah L88: ### Dr. Abdulrahim Ahmad Al-Assaslah
L89: 
L90: Imam & Khateeb – Al-Nahda Center, Chicago
L91: Dr. Abdulrahim Al-Assaslah was born in Jordan in 1963 where he received his early education. He earned his bachelor’s degree from Al-Zaytuna University in Tunisia in 1987, followed by a Master’s degree in 2008 and a Ph.D. in Islamic Jurisprudence and its Principles in 2015.
L92: Over the course of his career, Dr. Al-Assaslah has held several prominent academic and religious leadership roles, including serving as Dean of the College of Sharia at Mutah University and acting as a senior religious advisor within Jordanian institutions. From 2018 to 2022, he worked with the Jordanian Ministry of Awqaf, contributing to religious education and community outreach.
L93: In 2023, Dr. Al-Assaslah began his role as Imam and Khateeb at Al-Nahda Center in Chicago, where he continues to offer guidance, sermons, and religious education to the local Muslim community.
L94: cite42†Image: Sheikh-Suleiman-Al-Rawafi L95: ### Sheikh Suleiman Al-Rawafi
L96: 
L97: Imam and Khateeb at Al-Nahda Mosque
L98: Sheikh Suleiman Al-Rawafi currently serves as the Imam and Friday Khateeb (preacher) at Al-Nahda Mosque, where he leads prayers, delivers sermons, and provides religious guidance to the Muslim community.
L99: He studied Media with a specialization in Radio and Television at Qatar University and holds a Diploma in Diagnostic Radiology from the University of Science and Technology.
L100: With over fifteen years of experience teaching Qur’an and Islamic sciences, Sheikh Suleiman has worked in various Qur’anic and religious education centers.
L101: In the United States, he has served as a Qur’an and Islamic studies teacher at multiple Islamic schools and centers.
L102: He has also held various positions and contributed to the efforts of numerous organizations and associations throughout the U.S. In addition, he serves as the Chairman of the Construction and Development Committee for Al-Nahda Islamic Center.
