This pass looked for people who left the United States, or U.S. military authority abroad, for Soviet territory in response to prosecution, an impending arrest, military punishment, or claimed political persecution. It separately tracked intelligence defectors and voluntary emigrants so that Soviet residence alone is not treated as political exile.

The live database was checked by exact name and alias. `live-profiles-before.json` is the read-only export used for the comparison. `screening-leads.json` records the candidate-by-candidate disposition and supporting sources.

## Best new-profile leads

### Vietnam War resistance

The clearest missing political-resistance group is the **Intrepid Four**: Craig W. Anderson, John Barilla, Richard D. Bailey and Michael A. Lindner (later Michael Sutherland). The four Navy sailors deserted the *USS Intrepid* in Japan on October 23, 1967 because of their opposition to the Vietnam War. A Japanese antiwar network moved them covertly to the Soviet Union, where they spent about a month and publicly explained their opposition to the war, before they reached exile in Sweden in December 1967. None of the four appears in the live database. Their Soviet stay was a transit stage in a longer Swedish exile, not permanent Soviet asylum.

Sources: [contemporary TIME report](https://time.com/archive/6647901/the-war-caviar-encomiums/); Carl-Gustaf Scott, [Swedish deserter study](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A1079025/FULLTEXT02.pdf); [later historical overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intrepid_Four) (use its cited scholarship, not the page alone, for publication).

### Earlier political and labor flight

**Eugene Dennis**, born Francis Xavier Waldron and also known as Tim Ryan, already has a profile but its biography begins with the 1947 HUAC confrontation. It omits his earlier California criminal-syndicalism case. Dennis was arrested and charged in 1930, went underground rather than serve the resulting jail sentence, and left for Moscow around January 1931 under a false passport. He returned to the United States in 1935 under the name Eugene Dennis. This is a strong structured-exile update, not a new profile.

Sources: Harvey Klehr and John Earl Haynes, [*The Secret World of American Communism*, chapter excerpt](https://marxism.suda.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/f9/2d/1fd4dd2b4c08b6cd8a9b5063f3e0/ba466ce1-9cd0-47b3-b825-c9ab68c3c98b.pdf); Don S. Kirschner's biographical account summarized in [World Biographical Encyclopedia](https://prabook.com/web/eugene.dennis/3767582). The sources differ between 1929 and early 1931 because they variously date his prosecution, underground period, and actual departure; January 1931 is the most specific account located for the Soviet move.

**Maurice Halperin** is absent. After a Senate internal-security investigation and the loss of his Boston University post, he left for Mexico in 1953 and moved to the Soviet Union in 1955 while facing the prospect of U.S. extradition efforts. His case is a credible exile lead, but any profile should state that espionage was alleged and denied, and distinguish flight to Mexico from later residence in Moscow.

Sources: Don S. Kirschner, [*Cold War Exile: The Unclosed Case of Maurice Halperin*](https://books.google.com/books?id=fywJycUadCEC); [declassified State Department/CIA record](https://www.archives.gov/files/research/jfk/releases/2022/104-10172-10111.pdf).

### Later asylum cases

**Wade E. Roberts** is absent. The Army private went AWOL from West Germany in early March 1987, received Soviet political asylum, and returned to West Germany in November 1987 to face military charges. Later reporting says he was cleared of desertion. This is a short, closed exile episode and a possible military-resister profile, although the evidence found does not establish opposition to a particular war.

Sources: [asylum report](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1987-04-02-mn-2132-story.html); [return and expected charges](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1987/11/05/us-soldier-leaves-moscow-to-face-charges-in-west/229f9ea3-abd2-463b-a831-a7bc6b17ba66/); [later acquittal report](https://www.upi.com/Archives/1988/02/28/Soldier-cleared-of-desertion-has-no-regrets/8254573022800/).

**Arnold Lockshin** is absent. The cancer researcher and his family received Soviet asylum in October 1986 after he alleged FBI harassment, threats, and dismissal for his Communist politics and opposition to U.S. defense policy. Contemporary U.S. reporting disputed or could not substantiate parts of that account. He is a defensible exile lead only if the biography attributes the persecution claim and records the dispute rather than presenting it as adjudicated fact.

Sources: [contemporary asylum reporting](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1987-04-02-mn-2132-story.html); [1992 citizenship follow-up](https://www.upi.com/amp/Archives/1992/08/09/American-defector-wins-Russian-citizenship/5451713332800/).

## Intelligence-related flight: confirmed history, separate editorial decision

The following people are absent and indisputably fled or defected into the Soviet bloc, but their fit with a political-prisoner archive requires an editorial decision because the precipitating allegations were espionage rather than protected dissent:

- **Morris Cohen and Lona Cohen** left New York through Mexico in 1950 after the atomic-spy investigation reached their network. They later operated in Britain as Peter and Helen Kroger, served British espionage sentences, and were exchanged to the USSR in 1969. Sources: [FBI file](https://vault.fbi.gov/Morris%20and%20Linda%20Cohen/Morris%20and%20Lona%20Cohen%20Part%2001/view); [U.S. counterintelligence history](https://www.odni.gov/files/NCSC/documents/ci/CI_Reader_Vol3.pdf); [Science Museum account](https://blog.sciencemuseum.org.uk/the-krogers-radio-transmitter/).
- **Joel Barr** fled Paris for Czechoslovakia immediately after David Greenglass's arrest in 1950 and later settled in the USSR. **Alfred Sarant** fled the United States through Mexico after the FBI questioned him following Julius Rosenberg's arrest; he reached Czechoslovakia and then the USSR. Sources: [PBS Venona account](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/venona/inte_19441114.html); [FBI Sarant file](https://vault.fbi.gov/rosenberg-case/alfred-sarant/Alfred%20Sarant%20Part%2081/at_download/file).
- **Edward Lee Howard** escaped FBI surveillance in September 1985, reached the USSR, and received Soviet political asylum in 1986 while wanted on espionage and flight charges. Sources: [FBI artifact history](https://www.fbi.gov/history/artifacts/edward-lee-howard-dummy); [contemporary asylum report](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1986/08/08/soviets-grant-asylum-to-fugitive-cia-agent/5e975d03-346f-4a18-b6f5-1207beffaf65/).
- **Glenn Michael Souther** left during an FBI/Naval Investigative Service espionage inquiry in 1986 and later received Soviet citizenship and asylum. Sources: [contemporary Washington Post report](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1988/07/18/ex-sailor-defects-to-soviets/026db635-f855-4087-8dec-333f2d89b5a4/); [Sandia counterintelligence case study](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1762656).

**William Hamilton Martin** and **Bernon F. Mitchell** are also absent. They defected from the National Security Agency in 1960 and publicly denounced U.S. surveillance and military incursions. Because they left before any known prosecution or arrest, they are better described as ideological defectors or whistleblowers than fugitives from a pending political case. Sources: [U.S. counterintelligence history](https://www.dni.gov/files/NCSC/documents/ci/CI_Reader_Vol3.pdf); [contemporary newspaper account](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn90051770/1960-09-06/ed-1/seq-1.pdf).

## Existing IWW profiles

All nine Chicago IWW defendants who failed to surrender in April 1921 are already present: Bill Haywood, George Andreytchine, Vladimir Lossieff, J. H. Beyer, Herbert McCutcheon, Grover H. Perry, Charles Rothfiser, Leo Laukki and Fred Jaakkola. Only Haywood is currently marked `in_exile`; the other eight lack structured exile fields even though their biographies range from confirmed Soviet residence to a bare fugitive statement.

The contemporary IWW report identifies the nine bail-jumpers but, at that moment, confirms Russia only for Haywood. Later scholarship says the nine took different routes toward Moscow and provides individual confirmation for Andreytchine, Lossieff, Rothfiser, Laukki and Jaakkola. McCutcheon and Perry remain supported mainly by the group account, so their destination should not be upgraded from fugitive to Soviet exile without an additional individual source.

Sources: [Industrial Pioneer, June 1921](https://www.marxists.org/history/usa/pubs/industrial-pioneer/Industrial%20Pioneer%20%28June%201921%29_0.pdf); [IWW chronology](https://archive.iww.org/about/chronology/4/); Dimităr Baev and Kostadin Grozev, [Andreytchine study and archival citations](https://www.scribd.com/document/203480518/Andreytchine-Eng2014); [Finnish-American labor history on Laukki and Jaakkola](https://www.peacehost.net/FinnLabor/US-FinnHistory.htm).

The live Haywood record also contains an erroneous `in_exile_since` value on his 1906–1907 Idaho acquittal case. That value was mechanically copied from the case release date and must be removed; his Soviet exile belongs only to the 1918 Chicago IWW case and began in 1921.

## Existing Gastonia profiles need correction before expansion

All seven men convicted after the 1929 Loray Mill strike already have profiles. The live biographies repeat that all seven fled to the USSR and substitute Robert Allen for W. M. McGinnis in the list of convicted defendants. The court opinion and contemporary sentencing report identify the seven as Fred Beal, Clarence Miller, George Carter, Joseph Harrison, W. M. McGinnis, Louis McLaughlin and K. Y. Hendricks.

The best evidence located says all seven jumped bail but only five went to the Soviet Union. A December 30, 1930 letter from Moscow expressly names Beal, Hendricks, Harrison and Miller. Separate evidence places McLaughlin in Moscow later, making him the likely fifth. A September 1930 report instead places Carter and McLaughlin in hiding in the United States at that time, while McGinnis is absent from the Moscow evidence. On the current record, Carter's exile flag and McGinnis's prose claim should not stand; McLaughlin's later Soviet presence needs a direct source attached when corrected.

Sources: [North Carolina Supreme Court opinion](https://app.midpage.ai/document/state-v-beal-3672480); [Harvard trial-transcript finding aid](https://hollisarchives.lib.harvard.edu/catalog/law00163); [Labor Defender letter from Moscow](https://marxists.architexturez.net/history/usa/pubs/labordefender/1931/v06n02-feb-1931-LD.pdf); [September 20, 1930 Associated Press report](https://cdn.manchesterhistory.org/News/Manchester%20Evening%20Hearld_1930-09-20.pdf).

## Screened out or deferred

- Buford and Palmer-Raid deportees were forced out by the United States; they did not flee to the USSR. The Kenyon Zimmer roster comparison previously found no missing profiles in that group.
- Lee Harvey Oswald, Robert Edward Webster, Morris and Mollie Block, and most names in the House Select Committee's 1958–1963 defector study moved voluntarily and were not escaping a known U.S. political prosecution. The study is useful as a control list, not as a list of political exiles: [HSCA defector study](https://www.aarclibrary.org/publib/jfk/hsca/reportvols/vol12/pdf/HSCA_Vol12_DefectorStudy.pdf).
- Harold M. Koch, Theodore and Cheryl Branch, and Victor Norris Hamilton were subsequently researched and published in the later continuation. Their profiles preserve the limited or disputed nature of the original political and coercive claims rather than implying a U.S. prosecution that the sources do not show.

No continuing exile, present asylum status, or exact start/end day should be inferred from these historical sources. Year or month precision should be used where the sources do not establish a day.
