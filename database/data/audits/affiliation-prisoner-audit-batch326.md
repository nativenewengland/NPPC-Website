## Date and source limits

- Bertha Elkins: the Call's original scan spells her surname Elkins; the NYT transcription uses Eltren. Both describe the same December20,1909 release and reception. One record, with both spellings. The five-day workhouse commitment is supported by the NYT report; its actual completion by the release accounts. December20 is derived from “yesterday” in both December21 editions. The exact commitment day and statutory charge are not invented. No birthdate is calculated from reported age19.
- Josephine Casey: a signed letter, published May4,1912, reports nearly a week in Kalamazoo County Jail. Her later April30,1914 letter describes organizing and travel, establishing subsequent freedom. Released=true with no fabricated release date; her later death must not extend incarceration statistics through1950. Birth1876 and1878? conflict, so no birth year is inserted. A secondary37-day total is held pending direct verification. Her1950 death year is retained at year precision.
- The 1914 arrests of Sigman, Stupnicker and Metz and their releases on bail form separate periods. Stupnicker's April6 date follows the court guide's explicit two days after April4.
- All eight signed a July1915 message from the Tombs after the May11 recommitments. August7 was the bail-order date; no exact physical release is asserted. The first1915 interval has only a release year. Holzer's charge was dismissed September13; he is excluded from the later seven-person trial detention.
- The seven returned to jail September19. The October9 contemporary report distinguishes Metz and Woolf's discharge on Thursday, October7 from the other five men's acquittal on Friday, October8. No prison sentence is inferred. Periods outside jail on bail are not merged into continuous custody.
- Sigman's birth1880/death1931 are year-only values from Cornell's presidential biography. Other vital dates, individual statutory sections and unverified prison relationships remain blank.
