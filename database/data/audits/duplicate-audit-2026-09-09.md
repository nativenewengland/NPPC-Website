## Implications for the 42 profiles without cases

- Carol Silver and Jesse Harris have high-confidence counterpart profiles with cases (Carol Ruth Silver and Jessie L. Harris). Adding another copy of those cases to the empty profiles would compound duplication.
- Robert Taylor (political prisoner) may duplicate the New York Eight profile named Robert Taylor, but the short tribunal-petitioner entry does not establish that identity.
- A profile without a case is not automatically an omitted imprisonment: a person may be documented as an exile, supporter or protest participant without evidence of arrest. Case additions still require individual source verification.

## Method and limitations

The snapshot came from read-only queries of the live prisoner and prisoner_cases tables on September 9, 2026. No public-page pagination or currently-in-custody filter was used. All rows, including the two profiles under review, were included in the scan.

Profile candidates were generated from normalized names, slugs, structured name components, aliases and parenthetical names; exact inmate numbers and photo paths; identical biographies; and fuzzy spelling matches. Names were compared within first-initial/surname blocks and through uncommon shared tokens. Large identical-biography groups were omitted as candidate generators because shared roster text describes many distinct people. No birth date alone was treated as identity proof.

There were 646 candidate profile pairs: 66 high-confidence, 69 possible, 145 reviewed without establishing duplication, and 366 remaining weak matches. The latter category mainly includes shared group biographies, unrelated names and initials; it is not a confirmed-negative classification. The scan can miss aliases absent from the data, heavily corrupted spellings and identities described under wholly different names. The high-confidence count is a conservative finding, not an exhaustive historical identity census.

Every pair within each multi-case profile was compared. Four pairs match all stored business fields exactly after excluding IDs and created/updated timestamps. A broader pass returned 151 pairs with charge-text similarity of at least 0.60, or two recorded dates within one day. The CSV separates identical rows, clearly different episodes in the existing records, and unresolved overlaps. Stored partial dates can use January 1 or the first of a month internally; equality of those placeholders is not proof of the same historical day.

Examples of legitimate distinctions include the two Calvin Fairbank imprisonments, repeated Golden Rule voyages, separate Moses Harman prosecutions, and different suffrage arrests. A single prosecution can also have pretrial and post-sentence custody segments. Overlapping state and federal cases must not be collapsed merely because the arrest or release date is shared.

The CSVs provide the review trail without copying the complete database or biographies into the repository. A future consolidation batch should reconcile sources and conflicting fields, preserve distinct cases and related records, choose a canonical profile, and preserve old public URLs before removing anything.
