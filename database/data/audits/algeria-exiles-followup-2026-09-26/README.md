# Algeria exile follow-up

Published September 26, 2026. This pass followed the Black Panther International Section in Algiers and associated political-fugitive networks beyond the Algeria and France records already in the archive.

## Added

- **Byron Vaughn Booth** — escaped the California Institution for Men with Clinton Smith on January 27, 1969; hijacked National Airlines Flight 64 to Cuba the following day; subsequently joined the Panthers in Algeria; lived principally in Nigeria until his January 26, 2001 return; sentenced to twelve years on May 17, 2001.
- **Clinton Robert “Rahim” Smith Jr.** — escaped and hijacked the same flight with Booth, then went from Cuba to Algeria. His reported November 1969 death is described with its evidentiary basis: Elaine Mokhtefi’s firsthand account of Eldridge Cleaver’s confession, the discovery of a body and Booth’s later statement. No exact death day is asserted.
- **Rosemary Woodruff Leary** — served a six-month drug sentence, helped arrange Timothy Leary’s 1970 prison escape, fled with him to the Black Panther International Section in Algiers and remained a fugitive until her 1993 surrender. Her physical exile ends at her documented secret return in 1976; her underground status through 1993 is stated separately.
- **Barbara Easley-Cox** — joined Donald Cox in Algiers in 1970, worked with the International Section, traveled to North Korea, later organized with Black U.S. service members in Germany and returned to Philadelphia in 1973. The profile explicitly states that no personal criminal charge was located.

## Existing profiles expanded

- **Michael “Cetewayo” Tabor** now has a February 1971–October 17, 2010 exile case, Algeria-to-Zambia chronology, sources and the correct acquittal-in-absentia outcome. His death date was corrected from October 26 to October 17, 2010.
- **Larry Mack** now records his late-June 1970 arrival through Cuba and his work preparing the International Section headquarters. No current exile or later-life outcome is inferred because the reviewed sources do not establish one.
- **Sekou Odinga** now records Algerian political asylum and International Section work from June 1970 until his estimated return around 1973. His birth date was corrected to June 17, 1944 from his own published prisoner biography.
- **Dhoruba bin Wahad** now records his brief 1971 flight to Algeria and return to New York by June. His birth date was corrected to June 30, 1944 from his case statement.
- **Connie Matthews** now has sources and a closed exile span through her 1993 death rather than an undated open historical span.
- **Timothy Leary** gained source links. A false exile span beginning at his April 21, 1976 parole was removed; his real 1970–1973 exile record remains.

## Screened but not asserted

- **Victor Martinez** remains without an Algeria exile date. A scholarly source says he was believed to have obtained refuge there, but no separate evidence located in this pass establishes his arrival, residence or outcome.
- **Bill Stephens, Dave Jacobs, Ian Black, Bill Perry and “Lily”** appear in an April 1971 International Section discussion, but that attendance does not by itself establish that each was an American political exile.
- **Elaine Mokhtefi** was an American anticolonial organizer and the Panthers’ key Algerian liaison. She was not fleeing a U.S. prosecution, so this pass does not create a political-prisoner/exile profile for her.
- **Southern Airways Flight 49 hijackers Henry D. Jackson Jr., Louis Moore and Melvin Cale** reached Algeria in November 1972, but the reviewed record describes an extortion plot rather than a political asylum or resistance case.

All new and updated entries were checked by exact name and alias before publication. No current asylum status, continuing exile or exact date is inferred from a directory-style reference alone.
