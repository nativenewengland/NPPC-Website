## Sources and interpretation

1. [Boston Slave Riot, and Trial of Anthony Burns (Fetridge, 1854)](https://archive.org/details/bostonslaveriot00conggoog), printed pp. 10, 16–17, 34–37, provides the original named arrest lists and actual jail commitments. The public archive OCR was checked against the scan for the relevant reports. Saturday is May 27, following the Friday May 26 rescue attempt. The roster gives Walter Bishop in one report and Wesley Bishop in another, and Walter Phoenix/Phinney. The collective complaint is an allegation, not a finding that every named defendant killed James Batchelder. Some accounts also disagree on how Batchelder died; no weapon or perpetrator is asserted here.
2. [New York Herald, June 7, 1854, p. 1](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ndnp/dlc/batch_dlc_laceflower_ver01/data/sn83030313/00271743099/1854060701/1285.pdf), “The Boston Fugitive Slave Rioters,” distinguishes continued no-bail custody, riot bail, discharge and pending examination. The indexed text was readable, but a direct local download and screenshot were unavailable. Its damaged OCR dateline is not used for exact release dates. Coupled with the original May jail reports, it establishes multi-day detention; it does not identify actual bail posting or complete terms.
3. [Theodore Parker, The Trial of Theodore Parker (1855)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/31298/31298-h/31298-h.htm), introduction pp. xvii–xx, identifies the subsequent federal defendants and explains that Stowell's indictment was quashed and the prosecutor entered nolle prosequi in the others. Morrison, Cluer and Bishop are named in these proceedings. This later federal disposition is distinguished from the initial state murder complaint and is not treated as an exact prison-release date or proof of an uninterrupted year in jail.
4. [Report of the Harvard Class of 1853, 1849–1913](https://archive.org/details/reportofharvardc00harvrich), pp. 46–51, confirms Browne's identity, birth date, May 26 arrest, Suffolk jail confinement, June 7 admission to bail, reduction to riot and absence of a grand-jury indictment. The accessible archive transcription was read. Contemporary reports' Albert J. Brown / A. G. Brown variants are distinguished from the full name established by Harvard. Later summaries that call the initial complaint an indictment are not followed.
5. [Harmony Grove Cemetery's own record](https://harmonygrovesalem.discovereverafter.com/profile/34271121) independently confirms Browne's DOB and DOD; the June 27 burial is not his June 24 death. [Schlesinger Library's family collection](https://hollisarchives.lib.harvard.edu/catalog/sch00161) distinguishes the son from his father, Albert Gallatin Browne (1805–1885). Harvard Magazine's 1832 heading conflicts with the class report, archive and cemetery and is not used.
6. [The Liberator, July 7, 1854](https://fair-use.org/the-liberator/1854/07/07/the-liberator-24-27.pdf), reports Cluer speaking at the July Fourth antislavery meeting about his recent imprisonment. This corroborates his abolitionist activity and freedom by then. No foreign imprisonment or later brief speech arrests are added as separate US episodes.

All profiles use the broad, supported abolitionist-movement context. Charges and alleged participation remain attributed. Black race is explicitly recorded in the original reports for Bishop and Phoenix; unspecified race is left blank for the others. No birthday is derived from a newspaper age, and no namesake obituary is used. No personal support site was verified. Source links remain research citations.

Boston coordinates 42.36, -71.06 identify the historical prosecution city approximately, using the [USDOT Boston reference](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/node/193165). They are not current residences. Suffolk jail is preserved in Browne's narrative; no unverified institution relation is assigned.

## Date and status safeguards

The profiles identify actual multi-day imprisonment but do not invent complete measured spans. Exact arrest dates are stored. Structured incarceration dates are omitted because the current model could combine an unknown release with a much later death date to calculate a false decades-long term. The source jail-entry chronology remains in the biography and case narrative. No sentence, bail amount or later case dismissal becomes time served.

All records have `in_custody=false` and no active trial status. Only Browne and Cluer receive `released=true`, based on documented later freedom. For the other four, the release flag remains false because the individual physical-release outcome is unverified; this does not mark them currently imprisoned. Their narratives state the uncertainty, and isolated model checks verify no current custody flag, ongoing incarceration chart, invented endpoints or duration.
