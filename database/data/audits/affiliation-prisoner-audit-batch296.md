## Josephine Kaczor: inclusion evidence

[*The New Leader*, March 15, 1930, page 3](https://www.marxists.org/history/usa/pubs/new-leader/1930/v10n06-mar-15-1930-NL.pdf), “Nazareth, Pa. Strikers Keep Fight Strong,” identifies Kaczor as a Brookwood graduate and organizer in the hosiery strike that began November 4. It names her among eleven defendants acquitted of rioting but assessed court costs: $150 for her, $30 for each of ten other strikers. It then reports that the strikers refused payment, were jailed, began a hunger strike and were released four days later. The complete original column was visually checked to avoid mixing it with adjacent articles.

This is evidence of actual multi-day custody, not simply an imposed sentence. Her case preserves the acquittal and distinguishes detention for refusing costs from punishment following a riot conviction. The individual identities of the other ten acquitted strikers remain unresolved; they cannot yet be added as named profiles.

The [February 1930 *Labor Age* photograph, printed page 25](https://www.marxists.org/history/usa/pubs/laborage/v19n02-Feb-1930-Labor-Age.pdf), explicitly identifies Kaczor fourth from the right. Its caption describes **overnight** detention in Northampton County prison. That separate short detention does not establish the facility or dates of the four-day episode, and is not combined into the new case. See `photos/CREDITS-batch296.md` for the full retained original and exact crop.
