## Evidence and its limits

Primary evidence within [Mayham, *The Anti-Rent War on Blenheim Hill* (1906), scanned book](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5d/The_anti-rent_war_on_Blenheim_Hill_-_an_episode_of_the_40%27s_-_a_history_of_the_struggle_between_landlord_and_tenant_growing_out_of_the_patroon_system_in_the_eastern_part_of_New_York_%28IA_cu31924030043131%29.pdf):

- Vroman: printed pages 56, 63, 69–70 and 80–82 (PDF pages 73, 82, 90–91, 105–107). Page 63 names him at Ira B. Rose's tavern, guarded by Oscar and David Howard. Page 69 explicitly places him in custody on Sunday. Page 81 records his denial of a weekend release. Page 82 quotes Judge Mayham about counsel ending the detention.
- Warner: printed pages 83–84 (PDF pages 108–109). The daughter's first-person family account supplies his name, the two places of confinement and the week at Schoharie. Her account treats her father's and brother's custody separately.
- The author devotes a chapter to disagreements between witnesses. Retrospective dialogue is not treated as a verbatim contemporary transcript. The admission day, release day, formal charge and sentence are left unknown. The late-week-to-Sunday chronology supports multiple days for Vroman without choosing among the disputed exact dates.

Searches for vital dates produced William Vroman/Vrooman cemetery and military namesakes, including an 1829–1908 Peaslee Cemetery listing, but no sufficiently verified identification tying those dates to Thomas Vroman's son and the named prisoner. The history describes its witness as about eighty in 1906, which does not establish a birth year. No dates were back-calculated from that age. No portrait was verified.
