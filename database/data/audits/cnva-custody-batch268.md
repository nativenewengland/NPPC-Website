## Corrections and uncertainty

- **Henry:** the August 1961 Freedom report describes concurrent one-year terms. The February sentencing date is supplied by AP; CNVA identifies the later January boarding and boat seizure. The already-recorded 12 months are unchanged.
- **Martin:** June 19, 1961 is supported by the Justice Department bulletin published June 30, before the competing July date could have occurred. The September 1962 report confirms later Ashland custody. Four-year conditional release and six-year unconditional discharge are different Youth Corrections Act limits, not evidence of years actually served.
- **Aarons and Swann:** sentence and actual custody are separate findings. Neither is assigned 90 days actually served from the appeal alone. A scheduled November 19, 1962 commitment appearance is not stored as admission. The indexed November 20 Aarons probation notice is not converted into a prison release endpoint without clearer custody context.
- **Swann:** November 22 is the protest date; the appeal places him on shore and does not establish that as his arrest date, so it is not stored as one. His WWII case is untouched.
- **Albany:** the original roster supplies individually named detainees, with a separate January support group. The release bulletin gives group dates and internally inconsistent weekdays, and one marcher had already posted bond; no January 16/17 date is assigned to an unnamed individual. Fred Moore has a separately documented Christmas release.
- **Hartford:** the firsthand bulletin supports the Saturday–Monday detention and charges being dropped. Candy Kricker and Candy Stephens are treated as one person; the account identifies her as Josh Kricker’s sister and John Stephens’s wife. The John Stephens account has an isolated June 1 reference, but its dated opening and main narrative identify May 31; the June 15 release is explicit.
- **Everyman I:** the legal docket supplies the 30-day sentence; the participant memoir supports extended actual confinement but describes a longer approximate period. No exact total is inferred.
- **Omaha:** firsthand and organizational accounts support the three additional prison terms. Existing suspended-sentence cases are preserved. A planned January 20 release mentioned in a contemporaneous Hennacy letter is not promoted to an observed discharge.
- **Name collisions:** Albany’s Allen Cooper needs a bridge to the existing Ray Allen Cooper. Boston’s David A. Reed is distinct from the existing Elaine defendant Dave Reed. Other common names are screened against names, aliases, slugs and biographies.
- **Counts:** the supplied text gives incompatible “43” and “50+” estimates and includes arrests, suspended sentences and repeat participants. The attached CSV has 66 lead labels including the additional Rhoads lead and aliases; it is not a lifetime count of distinct jailed CNVA members. No unverified organization-wide total is published.
- New entries have no invented birth/death dates, portraits, or support websites. Archival group photographs are not attached without an unambiguous individual identification.
